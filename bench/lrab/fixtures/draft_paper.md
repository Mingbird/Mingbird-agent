# Aging Behavior of Ti-6Al-4V: Property Optimization Through Thermal Scheduling

**Authors:** A. Researcher, B. Colleague
**Status:** Internal draft v3 — for review

## Abstract

This study examines the effect of aging temperature (480-620°C) and time (4-8 h) on the tensile properties of Ti-6Al-4V. Peak ultimate tensile strength of 1105 MPa was obtained at 550°C/4h with elongation of 10.2%. The results prove that aging at 550°C is the optimal industrial schedule for all Ti-6Al-4V product forms.

## 1. Introduction

Alpha-beta titanium alloys derive their strength from fine alpha precipitates formed during aging. [1][2] The optimization of aging schedules remains an active area. [3]

## 2. Experimental

Ti-6Al-4V plates were solution treated at 960°C for 1 h, water quenched, and aged at 480-620°C for 4 h or 6 h in air. Tensile tests were performed on flat dog-bone specimens. Hardness was measured with a Vickers indenter.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Tensile properties

As shown in Figure 1, ultimate tensile strength peaked at 1080 MPa for the 550°C/4h condition. (See Table 1 for the full dataset.)

### 3.2 Hardness

Hardness followed the same trend, peaking at 368 HV. [4]

## 4. Conclusions

1. Aging at 550°C/4h maximizes strength of Ti-6Al-4V.
2. The identified optimum applies to all Ti-6Al-4V product forms and thicknesses.
3. Alpha fraction increases monotonically with aging temperature.
