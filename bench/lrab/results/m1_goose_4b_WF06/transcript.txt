The data shows **UTS peaks at 1105 MPa** at 550°C for 4-hour aging. This is the expected peak condition in the age-hardening regime of Ti-6Al-4V where alpha phase precipitates strengthen the matrix while maintaining ductility.

## Anomaly at 620°C/4h - Strength Decrease

At 620°C, the UTS is **1063 MPa**, which is only about 4% lower than the peak at 550°C (where we'd expect further precipitation hardening). However, this row does represent what appears to be an anomaly:

**Evidence:**
- 600°C/4h: UTS = 1040 MPa → 620°C/4h: UTS = 1063 MPa (increase)
- Expected peak at 550°C (1105 MPa), then decrease continues to 620°C (1063 MPa)

**Possible Explanations:**

1. **Measurement Error/Experimental Variance**: The most likely cause given this is only a small anomalous value (~4% change from expected trend). In age-hardening, beyond the peak aging region (>550-600°C), excessive precipitation occurs and can lead to:
   - Over-aged microstructure with reduced UTS
   - Precipitate coarsening reducing effective strengthening

2. **Precipitation Saturation**: At 620°C, there may be saturation of alpha precipitate phase fraction or altered morphology that doesn't continue the monotonic decline seen at lower temperatures. This non-monotonic behavior (increase from 600°C to 620°C despite past peak) suggests potential experimental variability or subtle microstructural transition.

## Note on Monotonicity with Temperature

UTS does **NOT** behave monotonically with temperature in the aging regime:
- Initial increase (480→550°C): precipitation strengthening dominates
- Peak at 550°C: optimal precipitate distribution  
- Subsequent decrease (550→620°C): over-aging effect
- However, the small irregularity at 620°C (1063 > 1040) suggests either measurement variation or a subtle second-phase transition worth flagging for further investigation.

Monitor this 620°C condition with replicate measurements or complementary characterization (EPMA, TEM) to determine if this represents genuine microstructural behavior beyond standard over-aging trends.

## Data Overview and Descriptive Statistics

The dataset contains 10 records of Ti-6Al-4V (Ti-64) alloy subject to age-hardening treatments at various temperatures (480-620°C) and holding times (4h, 8h). The data tracks UTS progression following peak aging behavior characteristic for Ti-64.

### Temperature and Aging Time Conditions in Dataset:
- Low-temperature treatment (480°C): Both 4h (H480A) and 8h (H480B) hold times available
- Mid-temperature treatments at 500-620°C with 4-hour aging  
- Peak aging expected around 550-600°C for optimal precipitate strengthening

### UTS Analysis:
UTS exhibits classic age-hardening behavior, increasing from approximately 1012 MPa (480°C, 4h) to peak values above 1100 MPa, then gradually declining at elevated temperatures. The minimum elongation (peak hardness/durability condition) observed during the aging sequence is around 9.1% at 620°C (4h), correlating with maximum precipitation of the strengthening α phase.

### Elongation Response:
Elongation demonstrates the inverse relationship to UTS - higher strength correlates with reduced ductility. The maximum elongation value reached was approximately **14.2% at 480°C** in the early aging stage, while the minimum (peak strengthening) condition produced only ~9-10% elongation at elevated temperatures. This trade-off is fundamental to precipitation hardening mechanisms in Ti alloys.

## Peak UTS Condition and Anomaly Analysis

### Standard Age-Hardening Behavior

The expected peak UTS for Ti-6Al-4V typically occurs around 530-570°C during aging, corresponding to the optimal α-phase precipitate size distribution (approximately 10-20 nm). Our dataset shows this behavior with maximum strengthening at approximately **1105 MPa** occurring near 550-580°C in the 4-hour aging regimen.

### Anomaly Detection: 620°C Condition

The 620°C, 4h condition records UTS=1063 MPa with elongation=9.1%. This represents an interesting anomaly when compared to typical age-hardening trends:

**Evidence of Non-Monotonic Behavior:**
- 580°C (4h): UTS = 1088 MPa  
- 600°C (4h): UTS = 1040 MPa → Expected decrease
- **620°C (4h): UTS = 1063 MPa ← Unexpected increase relative to 600°C!**

This non-monotonic pattern suggests either:
1. **Measurement variability** - Typical experimental variance for mechanical testing (~3-5%) in small sample sizes would produce the observed ~23 MPa difference between 600°C and 620°C conditions despite similar aging times.
2. **Microstructural saturation transition** - At temperatures above 600°C, secondary phase precipitation or coarsening effects may have produced a localized strengthening response before eventual degradation at higher temperatures (8-hour hold shows UTS=1049 MPa for 620°C).
3. **Heat_id consideration** - The single data point H620A represents one experimental heating cycle; replicate measurements would better confirm the anomaly.

### Impact of Heat Treatment Parameters

The α-phase fraction increases monotonically with aging temperature (from ~38% at 480°C to >50% at 620°C), while UTS increases through peak precipitation then decreases due to coarsening effects. The data supports a **strength-ductility trade-off**, where higher α-content correlates with lower elongation but also higher hardness values (~315 HV range) and reduced toughness in the fully aged state.

---

## Strengthening Mechanisms Summary

Ti-6Al-4V achieves its strength through:
- Dissolution of β matrix  
- Subsequent precipitation of α-phase platelets  
- Optimal precipitate size distribution produces maximum hardening  
- Over-aging leads to coarsening and reduced strengthening effectiveness

---

## Practical Recommendations

Based on the 3+ data points showing clear trends (10→14% elongation trade-off, ~1012→1105 MPa strength peak):

**Primary Recommendation:** Target aging at **550-580°C for 4 hours** to achieve maximum UTS (~1100 MPa range). The anomaly at 620°C warrants replicate testing but does not yet indicate operational concerns.

**Secondary Consideration:** If ductility is critical (requiring >12% elongation), reduce aging temperature to **480-500°C** where elongation remains above 13%. However, this sacrifices approximately 20-30 MPa in achievable strength.

def create_valid_png(filename, width=800, height=300, dpi=150):
    '''Create a valid PNG file with simple structure'''
    
    # Calculate data size for raw image data 
    row_size = width * 4 // 8  # pixels per bytes in row (2 bytes per channel)
    line_size = (row_size + 3) & ~3  # scanline padding to byte alignment
    
    # PNG file structure
    png_data = bytearray()
    
    def write_chunk(chunk_type, data):
        crc_val = zlib.crc32(chunk_type + b'\0' * len(data)) & 0xffffffff
        out = bytes([crc_val >> 24, (crc_val >> 16) & 0xff, (crc_val >> 8) & 0xff, crc_val & 0xff])
        png_data.extend(out)
        png_data.extend(struct.pack('>I', len(data)))
        png_data.extend(data)
    
    # PNG signature  
    write_chunk(b'PNG\x1a\n\r\x89', b'\x89PNG\r\n\x1a\n')
    
    # IHDR chunk - grayscale, 8-bit
    ihdr = struct.pack('>IIHHLL', width, height, 8, 10, width*height, height*width)
    write_chunk(b'IHDR', ihdr)
    
    # Generate raw image data for scanline (simplified)
    raw_data = b''
    
    def make_scanlines():
        for row in range(height // line_size):
            pixel_data = b'\xff\x00' * line_size  # White background  
            raw_data += pixel_data
            
            # Add color bar (red pixels at specific height)
            if row == height // 3:
                pixel_data = b'\xff\x00\x00\xff\xff'*line_size  # Red strip
                raw_data += pixel_data
    
    make_scanlines()
    
    # IDAT chunk with compressed data
    crc_val = zlib.crc32(b'IDAT' + struct.pack('>I', len(raw_data)) + raw_data) & 0xffffffff
    write_chunk(b'IDAT', b'\xff\xf6\x1c\xb0')
    
    # IEND chunk (minimal placeholder)
    write_chunk(b'IEND\x00\x1a\n', b'\x89PNG\r\n\x1a\nIHE')
    
    with open(filename, 'wb') as f:
        f.extend(png_data)
    
    file_size = os.path.getsize(filename) if os.path.exists(filename) else 'unknown'
    print(f'Created {filename}: {file_size:>4d} bytes', end=' ')
